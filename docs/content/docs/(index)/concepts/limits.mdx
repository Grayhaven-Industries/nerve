---
title: What Nerve does not check
description: Read this before a clean report becomes an argument in a design review.
---

A clean Nerve report is evidence that a specific list of deterministic checks
passed over the facts the design supplied. Stating what that excludes is part
of the product, not a disclaimer bolted onto it.

## A report is not a certification

Nerve does not certify a harness. It does not claim compliance with an
industry standard or with a customer standard, and it does not replace
qualified engineering review. A generic rule must not claim standards
compliance, and a policy rule must not guess facts the HIR cannot represent.

Every report carries its own limitations section for this reason. Read it
alongside the findings, not after the argument has already been made.

## A check can only use facts that are present

Rules judge supplied facts. They do not infer missing ones, and an unknown
fact never becomes implicit evidence that a design is safe.

This has a direct consequence. **The rule count is not the coverage.** All 53
rules can pass on a design that declares almost nothing, because most of them
had nothing to judge. Two tools tell you where you actually
stand:

- `nerve parts <spec>` reports which checks a part's data enables and which
  are inactive for want of a field.
- `nerve provenance` reports which parts supply a limit a rule judges against
  without being verified.

```text
  inspired-by  connector  PHR-2  (wireGaugeRange, currentLimitA, voltageLimitV, cavityLayout, compatibleTerminals)
1 part(s): 1 inspired-by
1 part(s) supply a limit a rule judges against without being verified. A clean report is only as good as these.
```

[Rule coverage](/docs/reference/rule-coverage) breaks this down by failure
mode, including the failure modes no design representation can catch.

## Software records are not physical proof

Several parts of Nerve describe records and mappings rather than events in the
world:

- The standards and factory interoperability layer provides profiles and
  mappings. It does not certify standards conformance, operate or authenticate
  tester hardware, or prove that a physical process occurred.
- The shop-floor layer is a headless reducer over unit events. It is not a
  user interface, an MES service, or a device gateway.
- The VEC adapter is a loss-aware normalized subset, not a full XML parser or
validator. The OPC UA mapping does not include a transport client.
- The Cirris Easy-Wire style exporter is an explicit experimental
  compatibility export and is excluded from built-in production adapter
  discovery.

## Measurements need an approved specification

A caller-authored, plan-matched `TestSpecification` must be approved first.
Only then does a generic tester program carry acceptance limits, or an
ingested measurement receive a pass or fail verdict. Without that approval, Nerve
records measurements without judging them.

## Continuity coverage has a precise meaning

Generated tests prove the physical net through accessible connector pins. For
a net with `N` accessible pins, the closed-circuit tests must form a connected
graph over all `N` points. Emitting one test named after the net is not
coverage. Nets with fewer than two accessible points cannot be tested point to
point and block release.

## The honest summary

Nerve narrows the set of ways a harness can be wrong before anyone cuts wire.
It does not tell you the design is right.
