## Design Context

### Users

Nerve serves harness, electrical, manufacturing, and design engineers who need to
review real harness data, trace every finding to its source, and produce dependable
build and test artifacts. The interface is a working engineering instrument used
for inspection and release decisions, not a generic dashboard or a certification
claim.

### Brand Personality

Calm, precise, and trustworthy. Nerve should feel rigorous enough for an engineering
review while remaining fast to scan. The voice is direct, factual, and explicit
about provenance, uncertainty, and limits. It should create confidence through
evidence rather than marketing language.

### Aesthetic Direction

A dark, instrument-grade interface modeled on Tokenbase's visual system and guided
by Linear's product principles: a near-black canvas, restrained lifted surfaces,
hairline dividers, crisp type, and a warm accent reserved for focus and meaningful
state. Use Geist for display, Inter for interface copy, and Geist Mono only for
code, identifiers, files, and numeric evidence. Hierarchy should come from weight,
tone, and spacing rather than shadows or excessive framing. Avoid generic SaaS
cards, bright AI gradients, ornamental motion, dashboard theater, and marketing
copy that competes with the engineering evidence.

Visual reference: https://github.com/tylergibbs1/tokenbase

### Design Principles

1. Put the harness evidence first; navigation and framing should recede.
2. Use calm density: concise copy, strong hierarchy, tabular numerals, and minimal
   chrome without hiding important engineering context.
3. Make provenance and limitations visible so every result says what it rests on
   and what it does not prove.
4. Prefer one clear workflow with plain language, familiar controls, and fast
   keyboard access for daily expert users.
5. Treat accessibility as structure: semantic controls, visible focus, adequate
   targets, perceptual contrast, and reduced-motion support.
6. Keep actions responsive and recoverable, with specific loading, success, and
   failure feedback.
